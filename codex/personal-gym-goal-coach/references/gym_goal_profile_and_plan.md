## Starting Strategy

The recommended starting strategy is a controlled recomp-to-lean-gain phase.

An aggressive cut to 170 lb is not recommended at the outset. At 6 ft 2 in and about 180 lb, losing another 10 lb could make the arms, chest, and legs appear smaller without producing the desired aesthetic build.

An aggressive bulk is also not recommended because it would likely add unnecessary body fat, especially around the waist.

The starting approach is therefore to:

1. Build muscle through consistent progressive resistance training.
2. Eat around estimated maintenance or in a small calorie surplus.
3. Keep protein intake high.
4. Replace most liquid calories with filling food.
5. Monitor weekly average body weight and waist size.
6. Adjust food intake gradually rather than making large changes.

Because the trainee is relatively untrained, gaining muscle while body weight stays fairly stable may be possible during the first several months.

## Initial Nutrition Targets

Use these targets for the first two weeks as estimates, not permanent numbers:

| Target | Starting amount |
|---|---:|
| Calories | Approximately 2,700 kcal per day |
| Protein | 170–180 g per day |
| Fat | 70–80 g per day |
| Carbohydrates | Remaining calories, approximately 300–340 g per day |
| Fiber | Approximately 25–35 g per day |
| Water | Roughly 3 liters per day, with more as needed when sweating heavily |

### Desired rate of change

- Aim for approximately 0–0.5 lb of weight gain per week.
- After the initial adjustment period, approximately 0.25 lb per week is a good target.
- Use weekly average weight rather than reacting to individual weigh-ins.
- If average weight rises faster than 0.5 lb per week and the waist grows quickly, reduce calories modestly.
- If average weight and gym performance remain flat for three consecutive weeks, add approximately 150 calories per day.

The initial goal should not be a rigid final scale weight. Appearance, waist measurement, training progression, and photos matter more. At this height, a more muscular body weight of roughly 185–195 lb may eventually look substantially better than 170–180 lb with less muscle.

## Daily Protein Shake

A basic daily shake can include:

- One scoop of whey isolate or another tolerated protein powder
- Water rather than milk
- Banana
- Oats
- Optional peanut butter

Protein powder and water are sufficient when only protein is needed. Add oats, fruit, or peanut butter when additional calories are needed. Avoid casually turning the shake into a 1,000-calorie drink unless body weight and performance fail to increase despite consistent tracking.

## Friday Restaurant Meal

Friday should have one flexible restaurant meal, not a full unrestricted cheat day.

Rules:

1. Eat the normal planned meals earlier in the day.
2. Keep protein intake high.
3. Choose one restaurant meal.
4. Choose either a regular soda or dessert, but not both.
5. Do not deliberately starve before the meal.
6. Return to the normal plan at the next meal.

Reasonable examples:

- Chick-fil-A sandwich, grilled nuggets, fries, and a zero-sugar drink
- Panda Express bowl with rice, grilled teriyaki chicken, and one additional entree
- A regular-size Jersey Mike's sub rather than a giant sub
- A Whataburger meal with one burger rather than multiple entrees
- A smaller Raising Cane's combo with a zero-sugar drink

The restaurant meal does not have to be perfectly healthy. The aim is to keep its total calories within a reasonable range and prevent one meal from turning into an entire high-calorie day.

## Soda Transition

Two regular sodas per day can contribute several hundred calories without providing much fullness or protein. Reduce them in stages:

### Week 1

- One regular soda per day
- One zero-sugar soda per day

### Week 2

- Zero-sugar soda only on normal days
- One regular soda with the Friday restaurant meal if desired

### Week 3 onward

- Mostly water and zero-sugar drinks
- Limit regular soda to one or two servings per week

When the soda calories are removed, some calories may need to be replaced with nutritious food to support muscle growth. The goal is better calorie control, not accidentally creating an aggressive calorie deficit.

## Training Framework

### Session length

Plan for 60–75 minutes per session. Longer sessions are not automatically more productive. Effective exercise selection, appropriate volume, hard sets, progressive overload, recovery, and consistency matter more.

### Recommended schedule

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Monday | Upper body |
| Tuesday | Lower body |
| Wednesday | Rest |
| Thursday | Upper body |
| Friday | Lower body |
| Saturday | Optional aesthetic-focused session |
| Sunday | Rest |

The optional Saturday session should emphasize shoulders, arms, upper chest, back width, and abdominal work without adding excessive full-body fatigue.

### Training-time recommendation

Begin with after-work training. Morning training would require an earlier wake time, and sleep consistency is currently a bigger weakness than training-time selection. Protecting sleep should take priority. If sleep becomes consistently reliable, morning training can be reconsidered later.

### Training objective

The primary objective is hypertrophy and appearance, but basic strength progression remains a useful way to verify that training is working. Technique should be learned first, and exercises should gradually improve through more repetitions, more controlled load, better execution, or additional productive sets over time.

## Sleep and Recovery

A practical sleep target is:

- Begin winding down at approximately 9:15 p.m.
- Be in bed around 9:45–10:00 p.m.
- Wake around 5:45–6:00 a.m., or early enough to leave for work on time
- Aim for 7.5–8 hours of sleep
- Avoid routinely relying on lunch naps to compensate for staying up late
- Keep weekend sleep and wake times within approximately one hour of the weekday schedule

Sleep consistency will directly affect training performance, hunger control, recovery, and the ability to maintain the plan.

## Progress Tracking Protocol

Starting immediately:

1. Weigh in every morning after using the bathroom and before eating or drinking.
2. Record the number, but judge progress using the seven-day average.
3. Measure the waist at the navel once per week under similar conditions.
4. Take front, side, and back progress photos once per month using similar lighting, distance, posture, and clothing.
5. Log all food and drinks for at least the first six weeks.
6. Log gym exercises, weights, repetitions, and sets.
7. Do not react to one unusually high or low scale reading.

Sodium, carbohydrates, food volume, digestion, restaurant meals, hydration, and bathroom timing can move scale weight by several pounds without representing a meaningful change in body fat or muscle.

## Core Principles

- Consistency beats an extreme short-term effort.
- Muscle growth is driven by productive training, sufficient food and protein, and recovery.
- More gym time is not automatically better.
- Aesthetic development still requires balanced training, including the lower body.
- A single restaurant meal or imperfect day does not ruin progress; return to the plan at the next meal.
- Adjust calories from trends over several weeks, not from day-to-day scale noise.
- The best target body weight is the one that produces a visibly more muscular physique while keeping the waist controlled.
