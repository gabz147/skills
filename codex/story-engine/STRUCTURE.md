# Structure — from premise to serialized arc

## The spine: GMCS

Every story (and ideally every chapter) is driven by:

- **Goal** — what the protagonist concretely wants.
- **Motivation** — why they want it (emotional root).
- **Conflict** — what stands in the way (person, force, self).
- **Stakes** — what they lose if they fail. Make stakes personal and escalating.

Write all four as one sentence each before outlining. If stakes are fuzzy, the reader won't care.

## Story arc (adapt, don't worship)

A reliable serialized arc:

1. **Hook / ordinary world under pressure** — open on change. Establish voice + the crack in their world fast.
2. **Inciting incident** (first 10–15%) — the event that makes the old life impossible.
3. **Rising action** — escalating attempts and setbacks; raise stakes and deepen relationships; plant payoffs.
4. **Midpoint turn** — a false win or false loss that recontextualizes the goal; the character commits.
5. **Complications & low point** — the worst version of the conflict; relationships fracture.
6. **Climax** — the protagonist makes the hard choice their arc was built for.
7. **Resolution** — short. Show the changed self/relationship. For ongoing serials, reseed the next arc.

## Chapter = mini-arc

Each chapter should stand as a unit: **something happens → something changes → it ends on a question.**

- Open with a hook (action, dialogue line, image, or tension — never throat-clearing).
- One scene, one value shift (+/−). Give the POV character a chapter-level goal.
- **Cut before the consequences resolve.** End on the "Plunge" — momentum that pulls the reader to the next part.

## Hook types (chapter & story openings)

- **In-media-res action** — drop into the turn, explain after.
- **Provocative line of dialogue** — a question, threat, or confession.
- **Disruption** — the normal broken in line one ("The wedding was off by noon.").
- **Voice hook** — a narrator so distinct the reader stays for the company.
- **Mystery / unanswered question** — withhold the one thing the reader most wants to know.

## Cliffhanger types (chapter endings)

- **Revelation** — a secret surfaces.
- **Decision point** — a choice posed, not answered.
- **Arrival / interruption** — someone walks in.
- **Reversal** — a win flips to a loss (or vice versa).
- **The unanswered text/door/call.**

Tie every cliffhanger to the **goal or main conflict** — cheap shock without stakes trains readers to stop trusting you.

## Serialized pacing

- Keep a **tension curve across chapters**: spike, small relief, higher spike. Never flatline; never max out every chapter (exhausting).
- **Pay off** planted threads on a cadence — give a real answer every few chapters so the reader trusts the long mystery.
- Romance/relationship arcs: stagger the beats (meet → spark → push-pull → near-miss → reversal → payoff). Delay gratification, but **deliver**.
- End "books"/arcs on a genuine turn, then open the next with a fresh hook.

## Tropes by genre

Lean into beloved tropes, then **twist exactly one** to make it yours.

- **Romance:** enemies-to-lovers, fake dating, forced proximity, second-chance, grumpy×sunshine, slow burn, forbidden love.
- **YA / teen:** new kid, secret identity, chosen one, love triangle (use carefully), found family, coming-of-age summer.
- **Fantasy / paranormal:** hidden world, bonded mates, prophecy, magic-comes-with-a-price, dark academia, fae bargains.
- **Thriller / drama:** unreliable narrator, the secret that won't stay buried, one-lie-snowballs, revenge.

**Twist methods:** swap who holds the power, subvert the expected payoff, give the trope a real-world cost, collide two tropes, or honor the setup but change *why* it works emotionally.

## Outlining workflow

1. One-line **logline** (character + goal + obstacle + stakes).
2. GMCS paragraph.
3. **Beat sheet** for the whole arc (7 beats above, expand as needed).
4. **Chapter list** — one line per chapter: its mini-arc + its closing hook.
5. Outline only the **next 1–3 chapters** in detail; stay flexible as characters assert themselves.
