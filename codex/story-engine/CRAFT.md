# Craft — prose at the sentence and scene level

The job of every paragraph: make the reader feel something and need the next paragraph.

## Show, don't tell

Don't *answer* the reader's questions ("she was nervous", "they were in love") — make the prose **imply** them.

- **Telling:** *She was furious.*
- **Showing:** *She set the mug down too gently, the way she did right before she stopped speaking to you for a week.*

Rules:
- Convert states into **behavior, body, and choice**. Anger → what the body does. Love → what the character notices and protects.
- Relationships are shown through **history and friction**, not labels. Reveal "what they mean to each other" through how they interrupt, defer, lie, or soften.
- Don't summarize emotional arcs in narration. Stage the beat that earns the feeling.

## Filtering verbs (cut them)

Filtering = narrating perception instead of the thing perceived. Delete *felt, saw, heard, watched, noticed, realized, knew, wondered, seemed* wherever the sentence survives without them.

- **Filtered:** *She felt the cold seep through her coat and realized he wasn't coming.*
- **Direct:** *The cold seeped through her coat. He wasn't coming.*

## Anti-AI-slop

Ban these phrases and their cousins; replace with something specific to *this* character and moment:

- "little did they know" / "little did she know"
- "in that moment" / "in this moment"
- "a testament to"
- "couldn't help but [smile/notice/wonder]"
- "sent shivers/a shiver down her spine"
- "barely above a whisper"
- "the air was thick with [tension/anticipation]"
- "a mix of [emotion] and [emotion]"
- "she let out a breath she didn't know she was holding"
- "her heart hammered/pounded in her chest" (where else would it be?)
- "a wave of [emotion] washed over"
- "little smile / small smile" reflex; "visibly [adjective]"
- adverb-stacked dialogue tags ("she said angrily") — let the line and action carry it

Also avoid: starting consecutive paragraphs the same way; ending every chapter with a one-line dramatic fragment (it's a tool, not a default); explaining the joke or the subtext.

## Dialogue

- Each character needs a **distinct voice**: vocabulary, sentence length, what they avoid saying. A reader should ID the speaker with the tag removed.
- Dialogue must fit the **situation** and carry **subtext** — people rarely say the true thing directly.
- Use **action beats** instead of adverbs to attribute and color speech. Mostly `said`; let beats do the work.
- Conflict in every meaningful exchange — someone wants something the other resists.

## Sensory grounding

- Anchor each scene in **2–3 concrete, specific sensory details** — not a catalogue. Specificity (the brand, the smell, the chipped tile) beats abstraction.
- Use the senses the POV character would actually clock given their mood and goal.

## Pacing & rhythm

- **Match prose speed to scene energy.** Action/tension → short sentences, fragments, hard line breaks. Intimacy/reflection → longer, flowing sentences.
- **Vary sentence length** deliberately. A short sentence after long ones hits like a slap.
- Don't skip or summarize important time ("Over the next week…") — if it matters, give it a scene.
- White space is pacing. Break paragraphs often; a single line gets weight.

## POV & voice

- Pick a POV (usually 1st person or close 3rd for Wattpad) and stay disciplined — only what the POV character can perceive and know.
- Filter the *narration itself* through the character's personality. Their bias, humor, and blind spots are the voice.

## Scene structure (every scene)

1. Enter **late**, leave **early** — start as close to the turn as possible.
2. The POV character has a **goal** in the scene.
3. **Conflict / complication** blocks it.
4. **Turn**: the situation or a relationship is different by the end (a value shifts +/−).
5. Exit on a **hook** into the next beat.
